1 The Six Pieces & How They Move

Each piece possesses a unique trajectory, valuation, and tactical role on the 64-square battlefield.

♚

The King

Priceless / Infinite Value
1 Square

Moves exactly one square in any direction (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal). The King can never step into check (a square attacked by an enemy piece). If threatened, the King must escape, block, or capture the attacking piece. If no escape exists, it is Checkmate.

Tactical Role: Vulnerable in early game; powerful attacking unit in the endgame.
♛

The Queen

9 Points
Any Distance

The most potent piece on the board. Moves any number of unoccupied squares horizontally, vertically, or diagonally, combining the full operational powers of both the Rook and Bishop.

Tactical Role: Devastating offensive power and dual-fork attacks. Guard her cautiously.
♜

The Rook

5 Points
Files & Ranks

Moves any number of unoccupied squares forward, backward, left, or right along straight ranks and files. Also collaborates with the King in the vital defensive maneuver known as Castling.

Tactical Role: Dominates open files, cuts off enemy Kings, and controls the 7th rank.
♝

The Bishop

3 Points
Diagonals

Moves any number of unoccupied squares diagonally. Each player starts with one light-squared and one dark-squared Bishop; a Bishop never changes the color of the squares it travels on.

Tactical Role: Long-range diagonal snipers. The 'Bishop Pair' is a formidable endgame advantage.
♞

The Knight

3 Points
L-Shape Jump

Moves in an 'L' shape: two squares in one cardinal direction and then one square perpendicular. The Knight is the only piece capable of jumping over other pieces, making it lethal in crowded closed positions.

Tactical Role: Master of unexpected forks (attacking King and Queen simultaneously).
♟

The Pawn

1 Point
Forward 1 or 2

Moves forward one square at a time, but captures one square diagonally forward. On its very first move from rank 2 (white) or rank 7 (black), a pawn has the option to advance two squares forward.

Tactical Role: The soul of chess. Pawns determine board structure and can promote into Queens!

2 The Three Special Rules of Chess

Three unique tactical mechanics that every competitive player in ChessHub must understand.

🏰

1. Castling (O-O & O-O-O)

The only move in chess where two pieces move in a single turn. The King moves two squares toward a Rook, and that Rook leaps directly over the King to the adjacent square.

Strict Castling Requirements:
  • Neither King nor target Rook has moved prior.
  • All squares between King and Rook are empty.
  • The King is not currently in check.
  • The King does not pass through or land on attacked squares.
Kingside (O-O) • Queenside (O-O-O)
⚔️

2. En Passant ("In Passing")

A special pawn capture. If an opposing pawn advances two squares from its starting square and lands directly adjacent horizontally to your pawn, you may capture that pawn diagonally as if it had only advanced one square.

Strict Timing Requirement:

En passant must be executed immediately on the very next turn. If you make any other move on the board, the right to capture en passant is permanently lost.

FIDE Article 3.7.d • Handled automatically in ChessHub
👑

3. Pawn Promotion

When a pawn reaches the eighth rank (farthest opposing rank), it immediately ascends into a Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight of the same color, chosen by the player.

Multiple Queens Permitted:

Promotion is not restricted to previously captured pieces. A player can legally possess multiple Queens on the board at the same time. ChessHub prompts an interactive piece selection modal upon reaching rank 8.

98% of promotions choose Queen (Underpromotion for Knight forks)

3 Victory, Draws & Termination Conditions

How games finish on ChessHub and how ELO points are awarded or divided.

🏆

Checkmate (1 - 0 or 0 - 1)

Decisive Victory

The King is placed in check, and there are zero legal moves to escape check, block the check, or capture the threatening piece. The game ends instantly without capturing the King. Full ELO gains awarded (K=32).

🛑

Stalemate (½ - ½)

Immediate Draw

The player whose turn it is has no legal moves, but their King is not in check. The game ends immediately as a draw. Even if one side is up 30 points of material, stalemate splits the score!

🔄

Threefold Repetition (½ - ½)

Repetitive Board State

If the exact identical board position occurs 3 times with the same player to move and the identical set of legal moves available (including castling and en passant rights), a draw is declared. ChessHub automatically verifies FEN strings in Cloud Firestore.

⏳

50-Move Rule (½ - ½)

Endgame Rule

If both players complete 50 consecutive turns without a single pawn move and without a single piece capture, the match may be concluded as a technical draw to prevent indefinite games.

🤝

Mutual Draw Agreement

Player Protocol

A player proposes a draw via the in-game dialog. If the opponent accepts, the draw is committed immediately to Firestore and both players share ELO calculations evenly. If declined, play continues without pause.

⚖️

Insufficient Mating Material

FIDE Article 6.9

When neither side has enough pieces to force checkmate by any sequence of legal moves (e.g., King vs King, King & Bishop vs King, King & Knight vs King). If a player times out but the opponent has insufficient material, it is scored as a draw.
